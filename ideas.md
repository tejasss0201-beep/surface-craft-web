# Surface Craft Exterior Care — Design Direction

## Three distinct approaches

### 1. The Restoration Journal
**Very Brief Intro:** A cinematic editorial treatment that frames exterior cleaning as a study in material, light, and renewal. Large image fields and restrained typographic contrast create quiet confidence.

**Probability:** 0.07

### 2. Gallery of Clean
**Very Brief Intro:** A bright architectural gallery where every service is presented as a curated exhibit. It feels precise, airy, and contemporary rather than domestic.

**Probability:** 0.04

### 3. Midnight Machinery
**Very Brief Intro:** A dark, energetic workshop-inspired direction with calibrated industrial detailing. The experience prioritizes speed, immersion, and technical texture.

**Probability:** 0.09

## Chosen approach: The Restoration Journal

### Design Movement
**Contemporary editorial art direction** informed by high-fashion lookbooks, architectural photography, and refined print journals.

### Core Principles
1. **Transformation as a visual narrative:** Every section should move from raw material to a visible sense of care, not just explain a service.
2. **Controlled contrast:** Vast chalk-white surfaces alternate with charcoal image wells, so the site feels cinematic without becoming theatrical.
3. **Measured asymmetry:** A vertical service rail, off-centre text blocks, and bleed imagery create an art-directed rhythm rather than a templated page.
4. **Tactile precision:** Hairline rules, soft grain, editorial captions, and performance-conscious motion reinforce craft at every scale.

### Color Philosophy
The foundation is **limestone white**, evocative of clean sunlit masonry, offset with ink-black for authority and cool mist gray for calm. A saturated mineral green acts as a scarce, intentional signal of renewal and action; it is used only when the visitor should notice a price, decision, or next step.

### Layout Paradigm
The site behaves like a **folding field journal**. A narrow persistent index on larger screens anchors the composition while sections unfold as varied spreads: diagonal image cuts, staggered content columns, oversized type beside small technical captions, and sequential scene changes rather than repeated card grids.

### Signature Elements
1. **Mineral-green registration marks**—small circular signals and rules that guide the visitor through content and interactions.
2. **Split-surface panels**—imagery and solid pigment meet at hard, architectural seams.
3. **Process captions**—compact uppercase notes, numbered in sequence, turn service details into an editorial story.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interaction should feel like examining a crafted folio: deliberate, physical, and clear. Hover states slowly uncover surface details, calculator choices move with tactile selection feedback, and each booking step foregrounds a single decision rather than presenting a form wall.

### Animation
Use opacity and transform only. Hero media settles into view through a slow reveal, sections enter in staggered 40–70ms cascades, and image panels lift by 6–10px on hover. Interactive UI transitions are 160–280ms with a crisp custom ease. Decorative movement, particles, and parallax are disabled under reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
**DM Serif Display** provides high-contrast, editorial headlines in a mostly upright style, while **Manrope** serves all technical labels, pricing, and body copy. Headlines are tightly tracked at large display sizes; body content is open and readable. Uppercase Manrope at 0.14em tracking forms the caption and navigation system.

### Brand Essence
**Surface Craft turns routine exterior care into a considered restoration experience for homeowners who notice the details.**

**Personality:** Observant, precise, composed.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are concise and sensory; CTAs are confident and specific. Microcopy gives helpful, calm direction without sales pressure.

> “Bring the original surface back into view.”

> “Price the restoration in under a minute.”

### Wordmark & Logo
The wordmark is rendered as a disciplined editorial lockup: **SURFACE / CRAFT** in two stacked, widely tracked lines. The logo mark is a bold circular pressure-wave symbol with a deliberate cut line, suggesting water moving across a restored surface without literal cleaning imagery.

### Signature Brand Color
**Verdigris Current — #19B978.**

## Style Decisions

All component and styling choices must reinforce the Restoration Journal direction: quiet luxury, asymmetric editorial pacing, limestone/ink contrast, and tightly rationed verdigris emphasis. Generic centered hero stacks, glossy gradients, excessive rounded rectangles, and default-interface typography are excluded.

The **SURFACE / CRAFT** stacked wordmark and circular pressure-wave mark must appear in the first viewport and recur as the page’s editorial masthead. Surface imagery must feature at least one restoration cue—water movement, a clean material edge, or a before/after-like contrast. Section labels, rails, captions, and verdigris registration marks must operate as a continuous field-journal navigation language.
